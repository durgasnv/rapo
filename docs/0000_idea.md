# rapo. — Project Idea

> **Less repetition. More connection.**

## What is rapo.?

**rapo.** is a web platform that helps organizers form fair, fresh teams for activities, events, workshops, and collaborative programs. Rather than assigning people randomly or repeatedly pairing the same participants, rapo. uses previous team history and event constraints to introduce people to collaborators they have not worked with before.

The name is inspired by **rapport**: the connection and mutual understanding that can develop when people work together. The platform encourages opportunities for that connection; it does **not** claim to predict personality compatibility or guarantee that teammates will vibe.

## The problem

When people register for group activities, organizers often form teams manually or randomly. This can lead to repeated teammate combinations, uneven group sizes, difficult last-minute changes, and time-consuming administration. Participants may miss opportunities to meet and collaborate with new people.

## Our solution

An organizer creates an activity, sets registration and team constraints, and invites participants to sign up. Before the activity, rapo. generates groups that prioritize **pairs who have never worked together**, while respecting group sizes and capacity. Organizers can preview and adjust teams; participants can confirm, withdraw, or be placed on a waitlist. The system handles replacements and explains when perfect novelty is impossible.

## Who is it for?

- College clubs, student communities, and hackathon organizers
- Workshop and training coordinators
- Volunteer programs and team-building event organizers
- Any community running repeated group activities

## How it works

1. **Create an activity.** An admin specifies its name, description, start/end dates, registration deadline, team-size rule, and optional participant capacity.
2. **Register.** Participants sign up while registration is open. When capacity is reached, additional applicants can join a waitlist.
3. **Generate teams.** At a configurable point before the event (default: three days before the relevant deadline), an admin initiates or approves grouping. The engine consults prior confirmed team assignments and current constraints.
4. **Optimize fairly.** The engine first tries to avoid repeat teammate pairs. If that is impossible, it minimizes how often people have previously worked together, subject to valid group sizes and event constraints.
5. **Review and confirm.** Admins preview and, where necessary, override groups. Participants receive assignments and can confirm or withdraw.
6. **Handle changes.** The platform offers waitlisted participants available spots, rebalances teams where allowed, and flags unresolved shortages for the admin.
7. **Build history.** Finalized team participation is recorded for better grouping in future activities.

## Event configuration

Support two important activity models:

- **Capacity-limited:** The organizer specifies a maximum number of participants and an allowed team-size range (for example, 3–5 people).
- **No fixed participant capacity:** Registration remains open until the deadline, with an allowed team-size range; the system determines how many valid groups can be formed.

The organizer may also set a fixed team size. When the participant count cannot be partitioned into valid teams, the system must clearly show the problem and available options; it must not silently create invalid groups.

## Important edge cases

- **Everyone has worked together before:** Minimize repeated pair frequency instead of promising impossible zero-overlap teams.
- **A participant has worked with everyone:** Place them where cumulative previous collaboration is lowest, subject to other constraints.
- **Too few participants:** Do not generate invalid groups; notify the organizer and suggest waiting, changing permitted sizes, or cancelling.
- **Odd or awkward participant counts:** Find a feasible mix of team sizes within the configured range, or flag infeasibility.
- **Participant withdraws before grouping:** Remove them from the active pool and offer the spot to the waitlist where applicable.
- **Participant withdraws after confirmation:** Attempt a waitlist replacement; otherwise let the admin decide whether a valid team may continue, needs rebalancing, or must be dissolved.
- **Simultaneous registrations or confirmations:** Use transactional checks to prevent exceeding capacity or assigning the same spot twice.
- **Admin overrides:** Permit manual edits, but validate capacity, team size, and duplicate assignments.

## MVP: what we will actually build first

1. User accounts and participant/admin roles
2. Activity creation and registration
3. Capacity rules and waitlist
4. Team history and a deterministic grouping algorithm
5. Admin preview and approval of generated teams
6. Participant assignment and confirmation/withdrawal
7. Basic replacement workflow and event status dashboard
8. Tests for grouping constraints and registration edge cases
9. A deployed frontend, API, and persistent database

### Future improvements (not MVP promises)

- Automatic grouping and notifications at scheduled deadlines
- Preference or skill-aware matching, without compromising fairness
- Multiple grouping strategies and measurable fairness scores
- Team analytics and collaboration network visualization
- Invitations, email notifications, and exportable reports
- Organization workspaces and more granular admin permissions

## Proposed technical direction

- **Frontend:** Next.js, React, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS
- **Backend:** FastAPI, Python, Pydantic
- **Database:** PostgreSQL with SQLAlchemy and Alembic
- **Grouping engine:** Python optimization logic; start with a testable greedy/scoring baseline, then consider OR-Tools for constraint solving
- **Authentication:** Secure session or token-based authentication with role-based authorization
- **Deployment:** Frontend on Vercel; API and background worker on a compatible hosting service; managed PostgreSQL

## What makes rapo. different?

Most event registration tools stop at collecting names, while random team generators ignore historical collaboration. rapo. connects **event management, collaboration history, constrained team formation, and real-world changes** in one workflow. The technical challenge is not just building forms: it is making team assignments fair, explainable, valid, and robust when participants join or leave.

## How we know it works

A successful MVP lets an organizer create an activity, collect registrations, generate valid groups, approve them, manage confirmations and withdrawals, and reuse the resulting collaboration history for the next activity. Automated tests verify that no participant is assigned twice, group sizes satisfy configured rules, and the algorithm avoids repeat pairs whenever feasible.

## One-line pitch

**rapo. helps communities build fresh, fair teams by bringing together people who haven't collaborated before.**
